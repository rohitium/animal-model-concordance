16 September 2026 · 1,949 human and 328 companion-animal programmes, crossed against 1,520 adjudicated results in 419 studies.

The premise holds, and it is slow

Of 799 classified drug pairs in the review, 477 run human-approval-then-veterinary-evidence and only 22 run the other way. Median gap: 30 years. Companion-animal medicine already adopts human drugs — unsystematically, and about three decades late. That lag is the whole opportunity.

Start here: the shelved assets

A human programme discontinued after Phase 2 or 3 has already paid for the expensive part — the safety package — and usually failed on efficacy against a human comparator or on commercial grounds. Neither necessarily applies in a dog. 35 such assets sit in areas where the review says dog biology corresponds.

Fasinumab

anti-NGF · Phase 3 · discontinued · Regeneron

The clearest instance of the thesis in the dataset. Anti-NGF is the mechanism Zoetis has built four companion-animal antibodies on — Librela and Lenivia and Portela in dogs, Solensia in cats — and canine osteoarthritis is the largest companion-animal indication there is. A human anti-NGF antibody that reached Phase 3 and was shelved is available in a way an approved drug is not.

The obvious objection, stated plainly: the human anti-NGF class was halted partly over rapidly progressive osteoarthritis and joint safety signals. Whether that liability transfers to dogs is the first question to answer, not a footnote — and Librela's own post-marketing safety discussion makes it a live one. The source list does not say why fasinumab specifically was dropped.

Two molecules removed from this table

UKONIQ (umbralisib) and GB5121 appeared here in an earlier version of this page. Both were withdrawn or halted over harm — umbralisib over a survival detriment in UNITY-CLL, GB5121 after patient deaths and a partial clinical hold. Neither is licensable inventory, and GB5121 sat beside the BTK argument below where it read as supporting evidence for the thing it undermines.

They were removed once discontinuation reasons were read from the source links rather than assumed unknowable. Of 387 discontinued programmes: 15 safety or withdrawal (now excluded outright), 67 efficacy failure, 87 commercial or strategic, 5 production or supply, 213 not legible from the source.

There is no BTK programme in companion animals. Canine lymphoma is held by Laverdia-CA1 (verdinexor, an XPO1 inhibitor). Ibrutinib and pirtobrutinib are both approved in humans, both unclaimed in pets, and the dog data already exists — published by academics, never commercialised.

Where to look: areas ranked on evidence

The review answers one question the market cannot: does this disease's biology correspond between dog and human? Areas are ranked on that, weighting how often findings agreed together with how much of the evidence is intervention outcomes rather than biological similarity.

Cardiovascular

Rank 78 · 41 candidates
13 of 19 dog results corresponded16 of 19 are intervention outcomes8 studies

The best evidence in the corpus and the only area where most results are level A — what happened when the disease was treated. Canine myxomatous mitral valve disease and DCM are common and spontaneous; feline HCM is the obvious indication for a cardiac myosin inhibitor. Unclaimed: mavacamten and aficamten (cardiac myosin), ivabradine, riociguat, macitentan, selexipag, apixaban, acoramidis and vutrisiran.

Oncology

Rank 57 · 93 candidates
93 of 107 dog results correspondedonly 5 of 111 are intervention outcomes34 studies

Most evidence by volume, weakest by kind: dog and human tumours look alike transcriptomically, which is a claim about biology, not about whether treating them works. Restricted to tumour types dogs get — lymphoma, melanoma, urothelial and mammary carcinoma, sarcoma, glioma. Myeloma, prostate and lung agents were cut for that reason.

Musculoskeletal

Rank 54 · 25 candidates
6 of 7 dog results corresponded5 of 7 are intervention outcomes3 studies

Good evidence on a very thin base — three studies. Canine osteoarthritis is the largest companion-animal indication and the most crowded: 25 programmes, four of them anti-NGF antibodies. Entering means beating Librela, not finding open ground.

Immunology and inflammation

Rank 47 · 15 candidates
16 of 17 dog results correspondednone are intervention outcomes5 studies

Highest agreement rate, lowest evidence quality: all 20 results are level C biological similarity. Atopic dermatitis carries nine companion-animal programmes — Apoquel, Cytopoint, Zenrelia, Befrena, Atopica — so the JAK and IL-31 routes are me-too plays into a proven market. The TNF biologics are unclaimed.

A claimed target is not a closed door

Crowding is market validation. Atopic dermatitis carries nine companion-animal programmes and osteoarthritis twenty-five — and Elanco entered atopic dermatitis against Zoetis's Apoquel and Cytopoint with Zenrelia and Befrena. 48 candidates here target something a companion-animal programme already holds, and the competition is named for each so a two-player field can be told from twenty programmes deep on praziquantel.

What this cannot tell you

“White space” means absent from the supplied 328-programme list, not absent from veterinary practice. That list is branded company programmes, not the formulary. The run initially surfaced furosemide and tadalafil as unclaimed — both are standard canine care. Common generics are now flagged, but the flag is hand-written and incomplete: unflagged means no company programme found, never not used in dogs.

Why an asset was shelved is partly recoverable, and 213 of 387 are still not legible. An earlier version of this page declared the reason simply unavailable. That was wrong: the source link usually carries it, and reading it removed two withdrawn-for-harm molecules that had been presented as candidates. Safety withdrawals are now excluded outright; efficacy and commercial failures are kept and labelled. Where the signal is not legible, treat it as unknown and check the molecule before acting on it.

No canine prevalence, market size or willingness to pay. No epidemiological source is held. The veterinary literature's attention is deliberately not substituted for prevalence — it measures what researchers study, not what dogs get.

Patent status is proxied only by approval age, and 301 of the pipeline candidates have never been approved, so the proxy does not apply to them at all.

Target-animal safety is not systematically checked. The exclusion list is reviewer-supplied. An absent flag means not checked, never safe.

The largest exclusion is 904 programmes dropped because the review holds no dog evidence for that condition area at all. That is a statement about where the review is thin, not about those molecules — renal, respiratory, dermatology and metabolic disease are all areas where a candidate could not be evaluated for lack of evidence rather than lack of merit, and it doubles as a retrieval to-do list.

Method

Two supplied programme lists crossed against the systematic review of animal-to-human concordance — 1,520 adjudicated results in 419 studies, 192 of them dog-specific — plus Drugs@FDA for brand-to-ingredient and approval dates, a condition-area mapping, a companion-animal target index and a species-safety exclusion list. Areas are ranked on evidence; molecules within an area are not ranked, because every candidate in an area inherits identical evidence and the only attributes that vary are stage, status, crowding and approval age — a blended score would have ordered them by how old they are while appearing to order them by evidence. Reproducible from x1_caninisation_candidates.py; the review is published at rohitium.github.io/animal-model-concordance.

Internal analysis for programme selection. Prevalence, market and IP inputs are absent and named above. Not a valuation.

## How candidates are selected {#selection}

Both legs of human evidence — safety and efficacy — plus either no companion-animal programme on the
mechanism, or a stop unrelated to clinical performance. An approval carries both legs. An efficacy
failure disqualifies, because demonstrated efficacy is the premise.

## What "no companion-animal programme" means {#presence}

A route says whether a *marketed product* works the mechanism — not whether dogs already receive the
drug, whether the condition is served by something else, or whether competitors hold patents and
have not launched.

Anti-IL-4Rα is the case in point. No IL-4R product is marketed for dogs, yet Merck Animal Health
holds a granted patent on anti-canine IL-4Rα with 2015 priority, Vetoquinol and Kindred Biosciences
have their own filings, and Elanco wrote off its IL-4R pet asset in 2024 for commercial reasons.

Each molecule is checked against the mechanism, patents and halted programmes against it, the
companion condition, the veterinary literature, and this review's pair records.

{{cand_presence}}

Only **{{n_nothing_found}}** of {{n_candidates}} return nothing on any check. {{n_mech_open}} have an
open mechanism inside a contested condition — no IL-4Rα competitor in dogs, but canine atopic
dermatitis is held by Apoquel, Cytopoint, Zenrelia, Befrena and five cyclosporine products.
**An open mechanism is not an open market.**

::: note What is not checked
**No register of approved veterinary medicines** — no machine-readable Green Book or EMA export, so
a drug approved for dogs outside the supplied list shows as "not found".

**Patents and halted programmes are searched by hand**, only where someone looked: Google Patents
blocks automated querying and the APIs that replace it need credentials. **A mechanism with no
patent entry has not been searched, and must not be read as free of intellectual property** —
anti-IL-4Rα read as open until someone looked, and four companies had filed.

A veterinary registry and a patent API credential are the two highest-value additions to this work.
:::

## Where the evidence is {#areas}

Areas ranked on how often dog and human findings agreed, weighted by evidence level and by how much
is treatment outcomes rather than biology. Oncology has the most evidence and the weakest kind;
cardiovascular the least and the best.

## BTK: evidence without a programme {#btk}

Three BTK inhibitors reached dogs at or before human approval and no companion-animal BTK programme
exists in the supplied list.

| Molecule | Canine evidence | What that evidence is | Human |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ibrutinib | Spontaneous canine B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma | A single dose-finding study | Approved 2013 |
| Acalabrutinib | Canine B-cell lymphoma, 25% response (5 of 20) | Single-arm, n=20, median progression-free survival **22.5 days** | Approved 2017 |
| Rilzabrutinib | Canine pemphigus | A sentence in a review; no trial design | Phase 3 **missed** its endpoint |

Thin, uncontrolled, short-lived: three weeks of progression-free survival is not a product. Not an
overlooked opportunity, then, but early signals nobody has taken to a registrational trial.

## What this cannot tell you {#limits}

- **No prevalence, market size or willingness to pay** — probably the largest determinant of value,
  and absent. Veterinary publishing attention is deliberately not substituted for it.
- **"Not found" describes the checks listed above.** The supplied list is branded programmes, not
  the formulary.
- **Patent status is recorded only where searched**; where none is shown, nobody looked.
- **Target-animal safety is not systematically checked.** An absent flag means not checked.
- **Molecules within an area are not ranked** — they inherit the same evidence.
- **Formulation, route, palatability and dosing** are not assessed.
:::

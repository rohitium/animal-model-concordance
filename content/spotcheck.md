# Spot-check this review

::: lede
Roughly half the results here were adjudicated by a single reviewer who was not blinded to the
pipeline's provisional labels. Rather than ask you to take that on trust, this page publishes a
fixed sample of results — with the source, the page and the sentence for each — so anyone can verify
them independently.
:::

## What to check

Open the paper, find the quoted sentence, then ask four questions **in order** and stop at the first
failure:

1. Is the quote really in this paper, and is it this paper's own result rather than a figure it
   quotes from someone else?
2. Does the statement say what the quote says — no more, no less?
3. Do the value, unit and denominator match the quote, and is the rate the right way round?
4. Is this genuinely an animal-versus-human comparison, with the right species and the right
   evidence level?

**Not failures:** paraphrase that preserves the meaning, rounding, or a page number one off from
where you find the sentence. **Failures worth reporting loudly:** questions 1 and 4 — those mean the
result should never have been kept, and they tend to come in classes rather than singly.

Two samples, drawn by fixed seeds so they can be redrawn and audited. Sample A is random across all
kept results; sample B is drawn only from the single-reviewer studies, and is the one to do first.
Reporting which question failed is more useful than prose: it says whether to re-extract,
re-adjudicate a category, or correct one row.

# Animal Model Concordance with Human Clinical Outcomes
## Systematic Review Protocol — v0.1 (draft for iteration)

**Date:** 2026-08-20
**Status:** Draft. Not yet executed. No conclusions have been formed.

---

## 1. Objective

Quantify how well results in animal models predict outcomes in human clinical trials, and characterize the conditions (species, disease area, model type, endpoint class, study quality) under which predictivity is high or low.

**Explicitly not an objective:** arguing for or against animal research. The output is a set of estimated concordance statistics with uncertainty intervals, plus an accounting of what the available data cannot answer.

### 1.1 Primary question (PICO-style, adapted for meta-research)

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| **P** (population) | Paired preclinical–clinical evaluations of the same intervention for the same indication |
| **I** (index test) | Efficacy or toxicity result in a non-human animal model |
| **C** (reference standard) | Result of the corresponding human clinical trial (preferably RCT, Phase II/III) |
| **O** (outcome) | Concordance: direction, statistical significance, and effect magnitude agreement |

### 1.2 Secondary questions

1. Does concordance vary by species (mouse, rat, dog, pig, non-human primate, cat, horse, chicken, ferret, rabbit, zebrafish, other)?
2. Does concordance vary by therapeutic area (oncology, neurology/stroke, neurodegeneration, cardiovascular, sepsis/inflammation, metabolic, infectious disease, pain, respiratory, ophthalmology)?
3. Does concordance differ for **efficacy** vs **safety/toxicity** prediction? (Prior literature suggests these are very different problems.)
4. Does concordance differ for **primary hard endpoints** (mortality, PFS/OS, MACE, 6MWD, functional scales) vs **secondary/biomarker endpoints** (LDL-C, HbA1c, amyloid load, tumour volume, cytokines)?
5. How much of observed discordance is attributable to preclinical **methodological quality** (randomization, blinding, sample size, power) and **publication bias**, versus irreducible **biological species divergence**?
6. Has concordance changed over time (pre-2000 vs 2000–2015 vs post-2015, i.e. after ARRIVE/STAIR-type guidelines)?

## 2. Two-track design

The literature supports two complementary evidence streams. We will run both and report them separately before any synthesis.

### Track A — Meta-research synthesis (systematic review of concordance studies)
Identify and appraise published studies that *themselves* measured animal-to-human agreement. Extract their reported concordance statistics, re-derive 2×2 tables where possible, and meta-analyse.

*Unit of analysis:* one published concordance study.
*Expected yield:* order 10^1–10^2 studies.

### Track B — Case-level dataset (our own paired analysis)
Build an intervention-level database of animal→human pairs with extractable quantitative endpoints, and compute concordance ourselves under a uniform definition. This avoids inheriting inconsistent definitions from Track A.

*Unit of analysis:* one intervention × indication × species pair.
*Target:* ≥ 150 pairs, ≥ 8 therapeutic areas, ≥ 6 species. (Number to be revised after scoping search.)

## 4. Eligibility criteria

### Include
- Reports an animal result **and** a corresponding human clinical result for the same intervention + indication, or an aggregate concordance/translation statistic across such pairs.
- Sufficient data to classify direction of effect and, ideally, effect size with variance.
- Any species, any therapeutic area.
- Regulatory review documents with paired animal tox / human adverse event data.

### Exclude
- Opinion pieces, editorials, commentaries, news, and narrative essays **that contain no original data or no systematic re-analysis**. (An opinion piece may be cited in a clearly-labelled §"Expert opinion" appendix, never in the evidence tables.)
- Animal-only or human-only studies with no counterpart.
- In vitro-only or in silico-only comparisons (recorded separately as a non-animal-comparator sidebar, not pooled).
- Conference abstracts without extractable numerators/denominators.
- Duplicate/overlapping datasets — deduplicated to the most complete report.

### Screening process
Two independent screeners (or one screener + one independent audit of a 20% random sample if resourcing is limited), Cohen's κ reported; disagreements resolved by discussion. PRISMA 2020 flow diagram with exact counts at each stage.

## 6. Analysis plan

### 6.1 Concordance definitions (all three reported; primary = D2)

- **D1 — Direction only:** same sign of effect.
- **D2 — Direction + significance (primary):** animal significant benefit → human significant benefit at α=0.05 on the pre-specified primary endpoint.
- **D3 — Magnitude:** ratio of standardized effect sizes (animal SMD / human SMD), plus whether human CI contains the animal point estimate.

### 6.2 Diagnostic-test framing

Treat the animal result as an index test for human benefit:

|  | Human benefit | No human benefit |
|---|---|---|
| **Animal benefit** | TP | FP |
| **Animal no benefit** | FN | TN |

Report sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, LR+, LR−, Youden's J, with Wilson 95% CIs; bivariate/HSROC meta-analysis across studies.

**Critical caveat to state prominently and quantify, not bury:** PPV depends on the base rate of truly effective interventions, which is low (Phase I→approval ≈ 10–14% in published attrition datasets — to be verified). A given sensitivity/specificity therefore implies a much lower PPV than intuition suggests; we will present a PPV-vs-prevalence curve rather than a single number.

### 6.3 The denominator problem (methodological centrepiece)

Compounds that **fail** in animals are usually never tested in humans, so TN and FN are systematically unobservable in the ordinary literature. Consequences: specificity and NPV are not estimable from journal literature alone. Mitigations, each reported with its own limitations:
1. Regulatory datasets (FDA/EMA) where compounds advanced despite equivocal animal signals.
2. Species-discordance natural experiments: drugs toxic in one species, safe in another, that still entered humans.
3. Repurposed/legacy drugs where human data preceded or ran parallel to animal work.
4. Explicit sensitivity analysis over plausible unobserved-TN ranges.

### 6.4 Bias correction
- Funnel plots, Egger's regression, trim-and-fill on preclinical effect sizes.
- Excess-significance test (Ioannidis–Trikalinos).
- Compare effect sizes from registered vs unregistered preclinical studies where available.
- Report pooled estimates **before and after** bias adjustment; the gap is itself a finding.

### 6.5 Stratified and meta-regression analyses
Pre-specified moderators: species; therapeutic area; model type; endpoint class (hard vs biomarker); phylogenetic distance to human; preclinical quality score; publication decade; funding source; sample size.

### 6.6 Heterogeneity
Random-effects (REML), τ², I², prediction intervals. Given expected I² > 75%, prediction intervals — not point estimates — are the headline output.

### 6.7 Pre-specified negative controls / falsification checks
- Human-to-human replication rate (e.g. Phase II → Phase III agreement) as an upper bound: animal models cannot be expected to beat the reproducibility ceiling of human trials themselves.
- Within-species replication (mouse lab A vs mouse lab B) as a second ceiling.
Concordance must be interpreted against these ceilings, not against 100%.

## 11. Rules of construction (enforced throughout)

1. Every quantitative claim carries n, the estimate, an interval, and a citation.
2. Evidence and opinion are physically separated into different files; opinion never contributes to a pooled estimate.
3. No conclusion is written before §6 analyses complete. Section headings will not contain claims.
4. Where evidence is contested, both sides' primary papers are cited together (e.g. Seok 2013 / Takao 2015).
5. Absence of data is reported as absence of data, not as evidence of no effect.
6. Prose is compact and numeric. No rhetorical framing, no advocacy language.

## 13. Known limitations to declare up front
- Specificity/NPV are only partially estimable (§6.3).
- Publication bias in preclinical literature is severe and only partly correctable.
- Endpoint non-equivalence between species forces judgment calls; the `endpoint_match` field makes these auditable.
- Selection into clinical trials is not random with respect to preclinical strength.
- Species coverage will be dominated by rodents; estimates for horse, chicken, cat, and great apes will likely rest on small n and must be reported as such rather than pooled into a headline number.

## 14. Open questions for the next iteration

1. **Scope of "outcomes":** efficacy only, or efficacy + safety? Safety concordance is a genuinely different (and historically higher-performing) problem. Recommendation: include both, report separately, never pool.
2. **Track B feasibility:** hand-extracting 150+ pairs is the largest cost. Alternative: restrict Track B to 4 deep-dive therapeutic areas (e.g. stroke, oncology, sepsis, cardiovascular) and rely on Track A for breadth.
3. **Database access:** do we have Embase / Web of Science / Scopus subscriptions? If PubMed-only, sensitivity drops and this must be declared.
4. **Veterinary angle:** naturally occurring disease in dogs, cats, and horses (e.g. canine osteosarcoma, equine laminitis) is a distinct and arguably more predictive model class. Include as its own stratum?
5. **Non-animal comparator:** include organoids / organ-on-chip / in silico concordance as a benchmark arm, or defer to a follow-on review?
6. **Target size and timeline** for each phase.
